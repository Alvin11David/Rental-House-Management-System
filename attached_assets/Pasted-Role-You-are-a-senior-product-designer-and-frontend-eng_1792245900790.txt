Role: You are a senior product designer and frontend engineer who builds award-winning, motion-rich web interfaces. Design and build a modern, attractive, web-based interface for a Rental House Management System used by three roles: Landlord (often absentee), Caretaker (on-site, collects rent), and Tenant. The product’s core promise is trust and transparency: every payment, repair, and agreement should be visible, verifiable, and hard to dispute.

Platform constraints: Web only (responsive browser app, no native apps, no hardware). Designed for Ugandan users: UGX currency formatting (e.g., UGX 250,000), mobile money context (MTN and Airtel), English with occasional Luganda terms, and mostly mobile-first use on mid-range Android phones with patchy connectivity.

Visual inspiration: Study award-winning interactive sites on Awwwards (https://www.awwwards.com/websites/web-interactive/). Borrow the quality of motion and craft, not the copy or layout. The interface should feel premium and tactile, but remain calm and trustworthy, because it manages people’s money.

1. Design Direction
Tone: Confident, warm, transparent. Think “a trusted ledger with a human touch,” not a cold bank portal.
Palette: One primary brand color for trust actions (deep teal or green), one warm accent for highlights (amber), and semantic colors used consistently: green for paid, amber for pending, red for late or overdue, blue for information. Support light and dark modes.
Typography: One expressive display face for headings and numbers, one highly legible sans-serif for body and tables. Use tabular numerals for all money and dates.
Surfaces: Layered cards with soft shadows, subtle glassmorphism only on overlays, generous whitespace on desktop, and thumb-friendly targets (minimum 44px) on mobile.
Iconography: One consistent set with clear status glyphs (✓ paid, ✗ late, ⚠ pending).
2. Signature Interactions (Awwwards-Inspired)

A. Immersive Property Creation Wizard (Landlord only)

Replace long forms with a step-by-step wizard (Property → Units → Tenants → Agreements → Review).
Use clip-path text reveals on step headings and slide transitions between steps with a visible progress rail.
Room image upload: a drag-and-drop zone that, on drop, animates thumbnails into a floating, staggered grid preview of the property layout. Show a skeleton state during upload and a clear retry on failure.
Autosave every step so a caretaker losing signal never loses work.

B. Interactive Floorplan and Room States

On the property map, each unit is a tappable tile colored by status (paid, pending, late, vacant, under repair). Landlord sees all units; Caretaker sees units only; Tenant sees only their own unit.
On hover (desktop) or press (mobile), the room scales up with a spring animation (stiffness ~300, damping ~20).
Clicking a room opens a right-side drawer with tenant information, payment history, open repairs, and quick actions. Never reload the page. The drawer closes with Esc, a swipe, or the close button. The drawer content depends on role: Landlord sees full details; Caretaker sees contact and payment status only; Tenant does not have this view.
Provide a list-view toggle for accessibility and for screen readers.

C. Kinetic Data Visualization (Landlord only)

KPI counters (total collected, outstanding, units occupied) count up from zero on load using easing, and reformat smoothly when values change.
Charts morph between Monthly and Annual views with animated line transitions, not hard redraws. Show a tooltip on hover or touch with exact UGX values.
Use GSAP or Framer Motion, but keep animations under 600ms and respect reduced-motion settings.

D. Context-Aware Cursor (Desktop, Landlord only)

During drag-and-drop assignment of tenants to vacant units, the cursor becomes a floating tag reading “Drop to Assign” over a valid vacant unit, “Move” over an occupied unit (with confirmation), and “Not Allowed” over an invalid target.
Touch equivalent: a floating label follows the finger, and invalid drops shake gently with a short haptic-style pulse where supported.

E. Fluid Client-Side Transitions

Single-page routing with no hard reloads. When moving from Overview to Tenant Profiles, metric cards slide down and fade out, while the profile list rises in with a staggered delay (40–60ms per item).
Use shared-element transitions where a tenant’s avatar or unit tile animates into its profile header.
Keep page state (filters, scroll position) when the user navigates back.
3. Unique Signatures (Make It Memorable)
“Receipt Stamp” moment: When a payment is confirmed, animate a rubber-stamp “PAID” with a subtle ink texture and a soft confirmation chime (muted by default). This is the emotional payoff of the whole system.
Trust Meter (Landlord only): On the Landlord dashboard, a small ring shows the percentage of rent verified digitally versus reported verbally, reinforcing transparency.
Timeline Ledger: Every unit has a scrollable timeline where payments, repairs, and messages appear as a continuous story, with each entry marked by who recorded it and when. Tenants see only entries for their own unit.
Ambient status bar: A thin top gradient shifts color subtly with the day’s collection progress (red at start, green at completion). Shown to Landlord and Caretaker only.
Sound-free by default, motion-rich by design: Every animation has a purpose. Remove any motion that does not clarify, reward, or orient.

5. Screen-by-Screen UX Requirements

For every screen, design these states for each role that can see it: empty, loading (skeleton), error (with a recovery action), success, and offline. Each screen must also meet the requirements in sections 4 and 6.

Landing and Login (all roles)

Role selection (Landlord, Caretaker, Tenant) before sign-in, with one clear reason for each role.
Phone-number login with OTP for low-friction access. Show a clear message if the network is weak.

Onboarding (per role)

Short guided tour (max 4 cards, skippable). Caretakers get a 30-second “Record a payment” tutorial.

Landlord Overview Dashboard (Landlord only)

Priority first: a “Today” card showing what needs attention (late payments, unresolved repairs, unverified payments) with one-tap actions.
KPI counters, collection progress, and the floorplan preview.
Trust Meter and daily summary card.

Floorplan / Property Map (all roles, scope varies per section 4)

Status legend always visible. Filter by status. Search by unit or tenant name (Landlord and Caretaker only; Tenant has no search).
Drawer for unit details (see section 2B).

Caretaker: Daily Collection (Caretaker only; Landlord sees read-only)

Built for speed on a phone. Large numeric keypad for amount, unit picker with recent units first, and a one-tap “Confirm” that gives instant feedback.
Running balance card (“12 of 30 collected, UGX 3.6M so far, 18 left”).
Offline queue: entries save locally and sync when connected, with a visible sync indicator.
Show the remaining unpaid unit list at the top so the caretaker knows who to follow up with.

Payments and Ledger (scope varies per section 4)

Table with sticky headers, search, and date range filter. Each row shows status, method (mobile money, cash, bank), and recorder.
Export to PDF or CSV (Landlord only; no Word files for end users).
Duplicate-payment warning to protect against double entry.

Tenant Profiles (Landlord full; Caretaker contact and status only)

Staggered list entry (section 2E). Profile shows contact, agreement dates, payment history, and open requests.
Sensitive data (phone numbers, IDs) masked by default with a reveal action that is logged.

Repairs and Maintenance (scope varies per section 4)

Kanban board: Reported → Assigned → In Progress → Done. Each card shows photos, cost (UGX), and timeline.
Tenants can report a repair with a photo in under 30 seconds.

Agreements (scope varies per section 4)

Clear summary cards: rent amount, due date, deposit, duration, and renewal date. Expiring agreements highlighted 60 days ahead.
Digital signature capture with a visible “signed on” timestamp.

Receipts and Notifications (scope varies per section 4)

Notification center grouped by Today, This Week, and Older. Tap to open the related unit or payment.
Receipt view with a shareable link and a print-friendly layout.

Tenant Portal (Tenant only)

Simple home: current balance (with due date countdown), pay-status, receipts, and “Report a problem” button.
Language toggle (English / Luganda labels for core actions).

Analytics (Landlord only)

Monthly and annual toggles with morphing charts. Export button. Plain-language insight line under each chart (e.g., “Collections are 8% higher than last month”).

Settings (scope varies per section 4)

Roles and permissions (Landlord only), notification preferences (SMS, WhatsApp, email), and theme toggle. Permissions shown as clear on/off descriptions, never jargon.
6. User Experience Standards (Apply to Every Screen)
Clarity first: Every screen answers “What is this? What do I do next?” within three seconds.
Feedback everywhere: Every action gives immediate feedback (button state, toast, animation). Never leave the user guessing.
Forgiving design: Confirm destructive actions, provide undo for at least 10 seconds where possible, and autosave drafts.
Plain language: No technical terms in the UI. “Payment not confirmed yet” beats “Pending reconciliation.”
Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA): Contrast ratio 4.5:1 minimum, keyboard navigation for everything, visible focus rings, ARIA labels on icons and status, screen-reader announcements for live updates, and captions or text alternatives for any motion-based cue.
Reduced motion: Respect the OS “reduce motion” setting by replacing animations with simple fades.
Performance: First meaningful paint under 2.5 seconds on a mid-range phone over 3G. Lazy-load charts and images. Compress room photos on upload.
Privacy by design: Show only the data each role needs. Tenants never see other tenants’ information. Mask personal identifiers by default.
Consistency: One design system with reusable components (buttons, cards, status pills, drawers, tables, empty states) documented in a shared component library.
